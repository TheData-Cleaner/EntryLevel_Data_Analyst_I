<h2>Project Overview</h2>

This project analyzes Udemy course data to identify patterns in course popularity, pricing, subscriber activity, and content characteristics across multiple subject areas.

The analysis focused on four course subjects: Web Development, Business Finance, Musical Instruments, and Graphic Design. Course performance was evaluated across factors including subscriber volume, course level, free versus paid enrollment, pricing, ratings, and content duration.

The goal was to determine which course subject represented the strongest opportunity to increase quarterly revenue and evaluate whether the available data supported increasing prices for Web Development courses.

<h2>Project Objective</h2>

The analysis sought to answer:

- Which Udemy course subjects are most popular among learners?
- Which subjects demonstrate the strongest revenue opportunity?
- How does subscriber activity differ between free and paid courses?
- Which course levels attract the greatest number of paying subscribers?
- How do course price and content duration vary across subjects?
- Does the available data support increasing prices for Web Development courses?

<h2>🧹 DATA PREPARATION: Udemy Course Data</h2>

<h3>Analysis Approach</h3>

The raw Udemy course data required cleaning and standardization before analysis.

The data preparation process included:

- Removing duplicate records
- Removing blank cells
- Correcting inaccurate course titles
- Correcting corresponding course links
- Standardizing column headers
- Standardizing the Web Development subject name for consistency across the dataset

The cleaned data was then used to create pivot tables and visualizations for comparative analysis across Udemy course subjects.

<h3>Analytical Value</h3>

- Improved consistency across the source data
- Created a standardized dataset for analysis
- Supported comparisons across course subjects and levels

<h2>📊 EXPLORATORY ANALYSIS: Course Performance</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

Which Udemy course subjects demonstrate the strongest learner demand and performance?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

Google Sheets and Tableau were used to create pivot tables and visualizations examining trends across Web Development, Business Finance, Musical Instruments, and Graphic Design courses.

The analysis compared course subjects across several dimensions, including subscriber volume, course level, price, ratings, content duration, and free versus paid course participation.

Web Development emerged as the most popular subject among the four categories analyzed.

<h2>👥 SUBSCRIBER ANALYSIS: Free vs. Paid Courses</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

How does subscriber activity differ across subjects, course levels, and free versus paid content?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

Subscriber activity was analyzed across the four course subjects and multiple course levels.

Web Development and Business Finance subscribers demonstrated similar patterns in their willingness to pay for courses, followed by Musical Instruments and Graphic Design subscribers.

Beginner-level subscribers showed the strongest willingness to pay across the four subjects, with particularly strong activity within Web Development.

<h3>Analytical Value</h3>

- Compared paid-course participation across subjects
- Identified course levels associated with stronger subscriber demand
- Evaluated differences between free and paid content
- Identified audience segments with potential revenue opportunities

<h2>💰 PRICING & CONTENT ANALYSIS</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

How do course price and content duration relate to subscriber demand across Udemy subjects?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

The project compared average course prices across subject areas and course levels while also examining average content duration.

Web Development courses demonstrated comparatively higher pricing and longer average content duration while also attracting strong subscriber demand.

These patterns suggested that Web Development learners were willing to purchase higher-priced, longer-form content. The original analysis proposed that learners may perceive these courses as offering greater value; however, additional research would be required to establish the factors driving that purchasing behavior.

<h3>Analytical Value</h3>

- Compared pricing across subjects and course levels
- Evaluated content duration alongside subscriber activity
- Identified potential relationships between course characteristics and demand
- Supported evaluation of a potential pricing strategy

<h2>📈 REVENUE OPPORTUNITY: Web Development</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

Does the available course and subscriber data support increasing prices for Web Development courses?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

Web Development demonstrated the strongest overall popularity within the dataset, with substantial subscriber activity among Beginner and Intermediate courses.

Based on the combination of subscriber demand, paid-course participation, course pricing, and content characteristics, Web Development was identified as the strongest candidate among the four subjects analyzed for further pricing evaluation.

The original project recommended increasing prices for Web Development courses, particularly within Beginner and Intermediate course offerings, as a potential strategy for increasing quarterly revenue.

<h2>🔎 KEY FINDINGS</h2>

- Web Development was the most popular of the four Udemy subjects analyzed.
- Web Development and Business Finance demonstrated comparatively strong paid-course participation.
- Beginner-level subscribers showed the greatest willingness to pay across the four subjects.
- Beginner and Intermediate Web Development courses demonstrated particularly strong subscriber activity.
- Web Development courses combined comparatively strong subscriber demand with higher pricing and longer content duration.
- Subscriber behavior varied by subject and course level, demonstrating the importance of segmenting course performance rather than evaluating Udemy courses as a single group.

<h2>💡 RECOMMENDATION</h2>

The original analysis recommended increasing prices for Web Development courses, particularly Beginner and Intermediate offerings, based on the high subscriber demand observed within these segments.

From a current analytical perspective, the findings identify Web Development as a strong candidate for a controlled pricing test rather than establishing that a price increase would automatically increase revenue.

A future analysis could evaluate price elasticity, conversion rates, historical price changes, competitor pricing, and learner retention before implementing a broader pricing strategy.
